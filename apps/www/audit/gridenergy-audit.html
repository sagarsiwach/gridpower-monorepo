01 · Executive summary

Where the site actually stands

The engineering scaffold is real and the honesty discipline on page bodies is genuinely good. But the site renders unverified specs in its own navigation, ships 22 blank pages as live routes, and has no working lead capture. By route count it reads ~54% done; by shippable reality it is closer to 30%.

What is solid

  • Two reference-grade templates locked: /solutions/homes/apartment and /products/nano.
  • Type-check passes clean (tsc --noEmit, exit 0).
  • Strong gating discipline in page bodies: 129 <Gated> guards across 25 files. No fabricated numbers ship as fact in content.
  • Configurator is honest by construction: never outputs price, payback, or kWh.
  • Active footer is correct: legal entity DeltaEV Mobility Private Limited, no public GridPower leak.

What is broken

  • The active nav fabricates. GlobalHeader + MobileSiteNav render unverified kWh bands and dead SKU names (Atlas-01, FlexCube 500SL) as plain menu copy on every page.
  • 22 live routes are blank stubs (return null) that 200 under a fabricated-spec header. Worse than a 404.
  • No lead capture. Contact, sign-in, configure all theatre. Backend 0%.
  • Copy discipline breached site-wide (em dashes in titles, descriptions, body).
  • No robots.txt, no sitemap.xml, zero OG tags.
The one thing to internalise

Route count is the wrong progress metric here. 19 of the 22 missing pages are clones of an as-yet-unvalidated template, and the highest-severity defect (fabricated nav) sits in two files that touch every page. Fix the cross-cutting defects and validate one enterprise spoke before mass-producing the rest.

03 · Method & evidence base

How this was verified

Findings below are grounded in the codebase as of commit fa6b2ec, not in the project's own status docs. Every claim cites a file and line.

  • Static scan of all 74 route files + component tree for banned terms, fabricated specs, and dead SKU names.
  • Chrome trace of root.tsx to confirm which header/footer/nav are actually wired site-wide (vs the legacy variants that only appear in previews).
  • Build verification: bunx tsc --noEmit → exit 0, zero errors. Dev server live on :5180, all routes 200.
  • Live render check of a stub route to confirm blank-body-under-header behaviour.
  • Asset existence check on mega-menu image references; SEO file presence (robots/sitemap/OG).
04 · Findings — launch blockers

P0 · must fix before any public exposure

P0F-01

The site fabricates specs in its own navigation

The wired desktop header (GlobalHeader.tsx) and mobile nav (MobileSiteNav.tsx) render unverified capacity bands and discontinued product names as plain mega-menu copy. The file's own header comment admits the numbers are not verified. The <Gated> guard that protects page bodies was never applied to the chrome, so these appear on every public page.

Evidence — components/site/GlobalHeader.tsx
42: (payback months, kWh, deployment counts). These are NOT verified... 128: name: "Apartment ESS", sub: "3–7 kWh · single phase" 157: name: "Large campus", sub: "500 kWh+ · multi-feeder" 136: label: "Datasheet · Atlas-01", meta: "Placeholder · verify" 167: title: "FlexCube 500SL", bullets: ["500 kWh containerised", ...]

Fix: Replace all sub-labels with audience names only (no kWh), delete Atlas-01 / FlexCube SKUs, map every tile to the canonical Nano / Micro / Mega / Giga classes. Mirror the same data object in MobileSiteNav. No number enters the nav unless it is sourced.

P0F-02

22 live routes are blank pages under a fabricated header

Every spoke and the micro/mega/giga product pages are 3-line stubs (return null) that the router serves as 200. A buyer who clicks a mega-menu tile lands on an empty body beneath a nav full of unverified specs. This is worse than a 404, which at least signals "not here yet".

Evidence — live render of a registered route
$ curl localhost:5180/solutions/homes/small-home → 200, empty <main> src/routes/solutions/small-home.tsx → 3 lines, return null (+ 21 more: large-home, factory, data-center, hospital, hotel, micro, mega, giga, …)

Fix: Either (a) build the pages, or (b) until built, remove their links from nav and route them through the catch-all so they render an honest "coming soon" rather than a blank 200. Do not ship a menu that links to nothing.

P0F-03

No lead capture — the site cannot do its one job

Contact, sign-in, and configure validate client-side and show honest pending states, but nothing is persisted. A storage brand whose "book a free site survey" goes nowhere is a brochure, not a funnel. Backend is at 0%.

Evidence
configure.tsx:5 "this NEVER fabricates price... collects qualitative answers" — but has nowhere to send them No POST handler / action / persistence in contact.tsx, sign-in.tsx, configure.tsx Tracked: Linear SAG-3350

Fix: Wire a minimal submission path (Cloudflare Worker + form action → email/DB) for the survey/contact form. This is the highest-ROI single task on the project; everything else markets toward a dead end without it.

P1 · fix before launch, not blocking internal review

P1F-04

Em-dash ban breached across titles, descriptions, and body copy

The house rule forbids em dashes (and --). The -- rule holds (0 hits), but em dashes appear in page titles, meta descriptions, and prose site-wide — the exact user-facing surfaces the rule targets.

Evidence — representative, not exhaustive
platform.tsx:42 title: "GridOS — the software layer — GridEnergy" HomeFoundation.tsx:36 description: "...the GridOS software that runs it — for homes..." contact.tsx:221 "No call required to qualify — share your details..." support.tsx:85 "...the issue is app-side — try reinstalling." Scan: 786 occurrences across 132 files (incl. comments; copy leakage confirmed in spot-check)

Fix: Either enforce the rule with a lint/codemod pass over JSX text + meta strings (replace with comma/colon/period/parens), or formally relax the rule. As written, it is being violated wholesale, which means it is effectively not a rule. Decide one way.

P1F-05

Component sprawl: 3 headers, 4 footers, legacy GridPower leaks

The active chrome is clean-ish, but the tree carries multiple superseded headers/footers/menus. They are only referenced by /preview and /system today, so they do not ship — but they still contain "© 2026 GridPower" and dead SKUs, and any accidental rewire re-leaks the dropped brand.

Evidence
ACTIVE: GlobalHeader + MobileSiteNav + SiteFooter (root.tsx) LEGACY (preview-only): SiteHeader.tsx ("Also from GridPower"), MainNav.tsx, footer/Footer.tsx, menus/footer/FooterFourCol.tsx ("(c) 2026 GridPower"), FooterUltraRich.tsx 45 GridPower mentions total; public-surface leaks are confined to legacy + /system showcases

Fix: Pick the canonical header/footer/menu set, move the rest to an explicit _legacy/ or delete. Reduces leak surface and the "which footer is real?" tax on every future change.

P1F-06

No crawl or share infrastructure

No robots.txt, no sitemap.xml, and zero Open Graph / Twitter tags across the site. Meta descriptions exist on ~38 surfaces but several "covered" routes are blank stubs. For a marketing site, share previews and crawlability are table stakes.

Evidence
public/robots.txt → absent public/sitemap.xml → absent grep og:image|og:title across src → 0 hits description meta: 38 files of 49 page routes (some are 3-line stubs)

Fix: Generate sitemap at build (route manifest already exists), add robots, add a default OG image + per-route og:title/description in root meta. One afternoon of work.

P1F-07

"Container" phrasing creeps toward the banned product name

Product names are locked to Nano / Micro / Mega / Giga, never "Container". The class pages use "containerised" / "multi-container" as descriptors. Defensible as form-factor language, but it sits one careless edit away from the banned noun and muddies the naming system.

Evidence — routes/products/index.tsx
76: "A containerised or room-scale system for factories..." 87: "A multi-container grid-scale system for power parks..."

Fix: Keep if intentional, but add a copy note so it is a deliberate descriptor, not drift. Never let "Container" become a tile label or product title.

P2 · quality & polish

Superseded audience pages

4 hubs (offices-industrial, institutes, enterprises, hospitality) predate the locked hub/spoke model and need rebuilding from the template. Currently live and inconsistent with /solutions/homes.

Homepage is a foundation, not a page

/ renders chrome + section scaffolding only. For an enterprise buyer this is the first and sometimes only surface; it needs a real narrative and proof.

Legal pages inherited, unreviewed

privacy / terms / cookies / disclaimer / warranty are short and unverified. A warranty page that states terms you cannot honour is a liability, not a placeholder.

Gating has no source-of-truth backlog

129 gates is good discipline, but there is no owned list of what number fills each gate and where it comes from. Gates with no plan to resolve become permanent vaporware.

05 · The strategic question

"Enterprise grid website" — is the architecture even right?

You framed this as an enterprise grid site. That reframe matters, because the current IA is built for a retail reader, and three things would change if enterprise is the real primary buyer.

The core tension

The IA is segmented by who you are — homes, offices, institutes, enterprises, hospitality: 5 audiences, 20 spokes. That is a retail map. An enterprise/industrial storage buyer navigates by load profile, product class, and procurement risk — uptime, warranty, financing, O&M, references. Today there are 20 consumer-shaped spokes and only 4 product pages. For enterprise, that ratio is backwards.

Three consequences if enterprise is primary

Decision pointRetail build (current)Enterprise build (if you pivot)
Primary nav axisAudience segment (apartment vs hostel vs hotel)Load / product class (Micro · Mega · Giga) + use-case
Depth investment20 thin audience spokes3–4 deep product pages with real datasheets
Proof surfaceOutcome cards, gated numbersCase studies, warranty terms, O&M SLAs, financing, references
ConversionBook a free site surveyRequest a proposal / RFP response / talk to engineering

My read

  • Don't mass-produce yet. 19 of the 22 pending pages clone an unvalidated template. If the audience axis is wrong for enterprise, you'd be cloning the wrong page 19 times.
  • Validate one enterprise spoke end-to-end first — factory or data-center — with real objection handling, before any clone run. Prove the convincer, then assembly-line it.
  • The gating discipline becomes a liability at enterprise scale. A buyer evaluating a 500 kWh+ purchase will not accept an all-placeholder spec sheet. Gates are honest, but enterprise needs them filled with sourced numbers — which is a content/ops problem nobody currently owns.
  • If you stay retail/SMB, the current IA is fine and the move is simply: fix F-01/F-02/F-03, then fill the stubs. The decision gates everything downstream.
Decision needed before more building

Who is the primary buyer — SMB/consumer across 5 segments, or enterprise/industrial by product class? "Fill the 22 stubs" is the right next move for the first answer; "restructure the IA around product class + proof" is the right move for the second. Building more pages before answering this risks 19 well-crafted pages aimed at the wrong reader.

06 · Remediation roadmap

Sequenced, not just listed

Ordered by leverage. The first three are cross-cutting fixes that touch every page; do them before any per-page work, because per-page work multiplies whatever state they're in.

1
P0 · ~½ day · cross-cutting

De-fabricate the nav

Strip kWh bands + dead SKUs from GlobalHeader & MobileSiteNav; map tiles to Nano/Micro/Mega/Giga. Removes the single highest-severity defect from every page at once.

2
P0 · ~½ day · cross-cutting

Stop serving blank routes

Until built, unlink the 22 stubs and route them through the honest catch-all. No menu link should land on an empty 200.

3
P0 · ~1–2 days

Wire lead capture

Worker + form action for the survey/contact form (SAG-3350). The site's purpose. Everything else markets toward this.

4
Decision gate

Confirm primary buyer

Retail-SMB vs enterprise. This routes all subsequent page work (see §05). Do not skip.

5
P1 · ~1 day

Copy + hygiene sweep

Em-dash codemod (or relax the rule), de-dupe legacy headers/footers, add robots + sitemap + OG.

6
P1 · ~1 day

Validate one spoke fully

Take the buyer-correct spoke (enterprise: factory/data-center; retail: a homes spoke) to final, with real objection handling. This becomes the proven clone source.

7
P2 · multi-day

Assembly line

Clone-and-fill the remaining spokes + product pages; rebuild the 4 superseded hubs; build a real homepage; review legal.

8
Parallel ops track

Source the gated numbers

Owned list mapping each gate → sourced value → owner. Enterprise can't launch on placeholders.

Critical path to "honest public soft-launch"

Steps 1 + 2 + 3 + 5 (≈ 4–5 focused days) get you to a site that is small but truthful and functional: clean nav, no dead ends, working lead capture, crawlable. That beats a 48-route site that fabricates in its menu and drops survey requests into the void. Ship narrow and honest before wide and broken.
